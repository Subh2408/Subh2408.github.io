For street photography, the goal is to look for **subjects first**.  
The street offers a mix of motion and stillness - and both can be equally compelling.  
It could be a moving cyclist, a stationary shopkeeper, or just the way shadows fall on a wall.

---
### Framing a Stationary Subject

Framing comes next.  
What you see with your eyes and what your camera captures - those are different things.  
You’ve got to **lock in to what makes sense to *you***. You’re not in the business of pleasing someone else.  

Say you spot a vegetable seller.  
That’s your subject. But what part of that moment are you trying to hold on to?

- The act of selling?  
- The seller’s expression?  
- The vibrant display of vegetables?

There’s no right answer.  
Once your main subject is clear, explore the secondary elements. Maybe it's the edge of the cart, the signage behind, or a customer mid-bargain.

---

### What Are You Trying to Say?

Now comes intention.  
Ask yourself - *What do I want this image to convey?*  
What should the viewer feel when they see it?

- Stillness? Movement?  
- Colour and energy?  
- Nostalgia? The beauty of routine?

The photograph can be loud or quiet, raw or composed - but it should reflect **what you felt** in that moment.

---

### The Everyday Is Enough

You don’t always need spectacle.  
Often, it’s the mundane that speaks the loudest:  
A tired vendor at the end of the day.  
A child watching traffic go by.  
Two pigeons fighting over crumbs.

These frames don’t shout. But they stay.

---

Photography isn't just about capturing what you see - it’s about making **the everyday matter**.
