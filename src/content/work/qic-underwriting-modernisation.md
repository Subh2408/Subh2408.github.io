QIC set out to move commercial underwriting onto a specialist underwriting
platform. I managed the programme across three cross-functional teams for about
four months. My work covered the business analysis, stakeholder facilitation and
delivery coordination.

Buying software is still a product decision. Somebody has to decide what the
workflow should be before the platform decides it for you.

## Mapping the process first

Before anything was configured, we mapped how a commercial risk moves from
submission to bound policy. The maps showed who touches each risk, where the
decisions sit, and where work waits. Those process flows became the reference
for every configuration question that followed.

## The business case

Leadership needed a clear answer to one question: what changes for
underwriters, and what is that worth to the business. The case rested on faster
turnaround on commercial submissions and more consistent risk selection. It also
gave underwriters more time for judgement and less for admin.

## Surfacing the risk

Buying a platform moves risk around. Some of it lands in integration with core
systems. Some lands in data. Some lands in whether underwriters adopt the new
workflow. I tracked project and implementation risk from the start and put it
in front of leadership as it emerged. The biggest was integration with the core
policy administration system, and with it the move of existing underwriting data
onto the new platform.

## The decision to stop

Leadership decided not to proceed before go-live. Stopping is one of the
outcomes a buy decision should allow for. A programme that makes its risks
visible early gives leadership a real choice.

## What I took from it

The next build-or-buy call I make will start with the process map and the
adoption risk. Those two things decided more than the feature list did.

Four months inside commercial underwriting also taught me the domain faster than
any course could. How commercial risks are assessed and referred now informs
every AI use case I look at in underwriting.
