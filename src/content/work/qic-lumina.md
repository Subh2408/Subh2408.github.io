Most people at an insurer are asked to hold a view on AI before anyone has
explained it to them. The knowledge exists. It sits in slide decks, vendor
briefings, regulatory documents and the heads of a few specialists. LUMINA puts
it in one place and makes it worth opening.

## The problem

Two gaps kept slowing AI work down. The first was vocabulary. Every use case
conversation started from zero, because people across the business had no
shared language for what AI could and could not do.

The second was domain knowledge running the other way. AI work in commercial
insurance depends on understanding how the business actually writes risk, and
that understanding sat with a small group of experts.

Both gaps were really one problem. Knowledge was scattered, and nobody had time
to go looking for it.

## What I built

LUMINA reads like a newspaper. Short articles on AI and on commercial insurance,
laid out so a reader can browse the front page or search for a single term and
leave with an answer. An LLM writes the articles from a vetted knowledge base of
insurance learning material. It also powers the search, so a reader who asks
about a term gets a plain answer.

The format was the main design decision. People read the news every day, so a
page that looks like a newspaper asks for no instructions. Each article is
short enough to finish between meetings.

## Getting to 200+ users

An internal tool only counts if people come back to it. LUMINA launched to
business stakeholders through internal channels and spread by word of mouth.
People came back because new articles kept appearing, and each one took a few
minutes to read. More than 200 people across the business now use it.

## What it taught me

Internal products compete for attention the same way consumer products do. A
colleague owes an internal tool nothing, so every decision about length, layout
and tone had to earn the next visit. Adoption was the metric that mattered, and
it shaped the product more than any feature did.
